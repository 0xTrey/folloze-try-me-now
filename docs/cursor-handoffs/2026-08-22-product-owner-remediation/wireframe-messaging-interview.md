# Three-Wireframe and Messaging Interview

Reply to all 30 in one response. “Agree” accepts the recommendation; otherwise replace it. These decisions govern the next Cursor package. They do not change the remediation wave already running.

## A. System rules

### 1. Selection: random or evidence-ranked?

**Recommendation:** Never random. Rank three approved wireframes deterministically from campaign motion, offer maturity, audience, proof, asset inventory, and brand density. Randomness makes weak output irreproducible and harder to QA.

### 2. Three wireframe jobs

**Recommendation:** Approve exactly these three jobs:

1. **Launch** — product, offer, event, or webinar promotion;
2. **Guide** — solution, industry, or category education/evaluation;
3. **Converse** — ABM or named-account conversation.

Are these the right three jobs, and what would you rename or remap?

### 3. Event placement

**Recommendation:** Treat event/webinar as a Launch subtype with event-specific proof and registration CTA, not a fourth wireframe. Agree?

### 4. Section count

**Recommendation:** Give each wireframe a reviewed six-section default, permit one optional proof section and one optional resource section, and never generate fewer than five or more than eight. Agree?

### 5. Internal selection visibility

**Recommendation:** Keep the selected wireframe hidden from the prospect; expose the selection name, factors, and confidence only in trace/QA. Agree?

### 6. Navigation

**Recommendation:** Use a sticky, compact section navigation with buyer-readable labels derived from the actual sections. Never use internal labels such as “account thesis,” “decision path,” or “supporting proof.” Agree?

### 7. First viewport contract

**Recommendation:** Every hero gets one company lockup, one specific headline, one supporting sentence, one CTA group, and one strong source-owned image or intentional type-led composition. No cards, tabs, receipt labels, or explanatory UI in the hero. Agree?

### 8. Brand translation freedom

**Recommendation:** Preserve seller logo, semantic color roles, typography character, radius, density, and imagery style, while translating them into an original Folloze page rather than copying the homepage pixel-for-pixel. Which brand cues are absolutely non-negotiable?

### 9. Imagery inventory

**Recommendation:** Require a minimum reviewed asset plan before final render: logo plus two distinct source-owned images when available. Prioritize product UI/product photography, then people/context imagery, then diagrams; reject sale banners, navigation icons, awards, stock filler, and duplicate crops. Agree?

### 10. Interaction budget

**Recommendation:** One primary exploration device per page (paths, comparison, use-case selector, agenda, or account questions) plus ordinary CTA/navigation. Avoid stacking multiple gimmicks. Agree?

## B. Wireframe 1: Launch

### 11. Launch hero promise

**Recommendation:** Lead with the buyer outcome or change the offer enables, with the product/offer named in the supporting line. Avoid “Introducing,” “Unlock,” and generic vendor announcements. Should the product name ever lead the headline?

### 12. Launch section 2: urgency/problem

**Recommendation:** State one recognizable buyer problem and the cost/friction of the current approach, using only sourced or clearly framed qualitative language. No invented market urgency. Agree?

### 13. Launch section 3: how it works

**Recommendation:** Explain the offer through a three-step mechanism or capability sequence tied to buyer outcomes, supported by product imagery or a source-derived diagram. Should this be interactive or static by default?

### 14. Launch section 4: use-case paths

**Recommendation:** Offer exactly three audience/use-case paths, each with a concrete job, benefit, and relevant capability. The paths must be derived from actual buyer research, not generic role labels. Agree?

### 15. Launch section 5: proof

**Recommendation:** Use the strongest available proof in this order: approved customer outcome, sourced quantified claim, product evidence/demonstration, then a clearly labeled validation plan. Never invent a statistic or customer. Agree?

### 16. Launch close

**Recommendation:** Close with one restated outcome, one primary CTA, and at most one secondary resource CTA. Default CTA is “Book a meeting” for product/solution launches and “Register” for events. What exceptions should be allowed?

## C. Wireframe 2: Guide

### 17. Guide hero argument

**Recommendation:** Lead with a sharp category or industry point of view: what changed, why the old approach breaks, and what better decision is now possible. The seller appears as the credible guide, not the subject of the headline. Agree?

### 18. Guide section 2: stakes

**Recommendation:** Frame 2–3 connected business stakes in one composed section, not a generic three-card feature grid. Each stake must resolve into a buyer question the page will answer. Agree?

### 19. Guide section 3: evaluation framework

**Recommendation:** Provide a named, buyer-useful evaluation framework with 3–4 criteria. Names must be plain language, not invented consulting jargon. Should Folloze coin a lightweight framework name or keep it unbranded?

### 20. Guide section 4: solution mapping

**Recommendation:** Map each evaluation criterion to the seller's real capability, product, or approach and show how it changes the decision. Avoid feature lists detached from the earlier argument. Agree?

### 21. Guide section 5: audience/industry application

**Recommendation:** Show three concrete scenarios tailored to the selected industry or buyer roles, each with a trigger, decision, and desired outcome. Agree?

### 22. Guide proof and close

**Recommendation:** End with evidence/resources that help the buyer evaluate, followed by a consultative CTA such as “Discuss your priorities” or “Book a working session,” not “Learn more.” Which default CTA fits Folloze best?

## D. Wireframe 3: Converse

### 23. Seller/target lockup

**Recommendation:** Use real seller and target logos only when verified, with a restrained “Seller for Target” lockup. Target brand never controls page colors. If the target logo is missing, use its normalized name without a fake badge. Agree?

### 24. Account hero

**Recommendation:** Lead with a relevant target-account priority or decision, then connect the seller's offer to it. Avoid empty “Company A helps Company B…” constructions. How direct should we be when account evidence is thin?

### 25. Account section 2: why this conversation

**Recommendation:** Present 2–3 sourced observations about the target and explicitly connect each to a seller capability or working hypothesis. Clearly distinguish public fact from inference. Agree?

### 26. Account section 3: shared opportunity

**Recommendation:** Build one joint opportunity statement with three practical workstreams, each owned by a likely buying-group role. Avoid grand transformation language. Agree?

### 27. Account section 4: buying-group paths

**Recommendation:** Let three target-specific personas choose a path. Each path changes the question, proof, and next step—not only the label. Agree?

### 28. Account proof

**Recommendation:** Prefer seller proof relevant to the target's industry/use case. If no approved proof exists, use product evidence plus a proposed validation agenda. Should a named-account page ever show generic company proof?

### 29. Account close

**Recommendation:** Close with a low-friction working-session CTA that restates the first decision to make together. No generic “Get started.” What should the default button language be?

## E. Copy-quality constitution

### 30. Final copy contract

**Recommendation:** Approve all of these as hard rules:

- headlines are specific, active, and usually 5–12 words;
- body paragraphs are usually 25–60 words;
- each section introduces one new idea and earns its place;
- name the buyer, problem, mechanism, evidence, and next step in concrete language;
- prefer verbs and nouns over abstractions;
- ban filler such as “operating outcome,” “business fit,” “decision path,” “supporting proof,” “seamless,” “transform,” and “unlock” unless source language makes the term necessary;
- no invented metrics, customers, quotes, deadlines, or superiority claims;
- copy that could fit a competitor unchanged fails QA;
- every image must support the adjacent claim, not merely decorate the section;
- final copy receives a separate adversarial edit for specificity, repetition, evidence, and headline quality.

Which rules would you change, and what additional phrases or behaviors should be banned?
