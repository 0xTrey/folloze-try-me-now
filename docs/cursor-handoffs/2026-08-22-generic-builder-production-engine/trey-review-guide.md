# Trey Review Guide

This is the product-owner review pass. It gathers judgment and defects; it does not assign Cursor's grade.

Local review: `http://127.0.0.1:3001/`

## Run three builds

Use a fresh session for each. Let the builder recommend the audience, offer, and objective at least once; make one explicit edit in another run.

| Run | Starting signal | Suggested direction | What it exposes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `adp.com` | Product or solution campaign for Workforce Now | recognizable red/black ADP system, offer research, recommendation quality, workforce-specific copy |
| 2 | `apple.com` | Product campaign for iPad or Mac | restrained Apple palette, typography and density, correct use of blue as an action color rather than a page color |
| 3 | `servicetitan.com` | Industry or solution campaign for service operations leaders | canonical identity, audience specificity, deeper page composition, useful proof and CTA |

Optional recovery check: enter a valid brand whose logo is difficult to retrieve. The builder must show an intentional typographic fallback—never a broken image, invented mark, or generic palette presented as verified.

## Review in this order

1. **Guided input:** Does it ask one consequential question at a time? Are the three recommendations genuinely useful? Can free-form input replace them without friction?
2. **Timing and honesty:** Is useful work visible immediately? Does the first usable preview arrive in under a minute? Do progress labels reflect real work rather than theatrical percentages?
3. **Identity:** Is the normalized company name correct? Is the logo real or the fallback explicit? Does the page clearly belong to the seller—not the target account?
4. **Brand fidelity:** Do color roles, typography, radius, density, imagery, and navigation resemble the public brand? Check the whole page, not only the CTA color.
5. **Buyer story:** Could the headline and body copy fit a competitor unchanged? Does the page name the audience, problem, mechanism, useful evidence, and next step without invented claims?
6. **Composition:** Does the selected 4–8-section journey feel intentional? Is the first viewport strong? Are later sections materially different rather than repeated headline cards?
7. **Interaction:** Do tabs, paths, cards, and CTA treatments help the buyer explore? Are there dead controls, placeholders, blank asset frames, clipped copy, or janky nested scrolling?
8. **Recovery:** Edit an earlier answer after research begins. The current answer must win; stale output must not replace the page or reset the session.

## Leave feedback

Use browser comments directly on the affected element. Prefix each comment with one label:

- `BLOCKER` — breaks the experience, identity, truth, or usable output;
- `MATERIAL` — works but misses the buyer-ready/on-brand promise;
- `POLISH` — a bounded visual or copy refinement;
- `KEEP` — a pattern that should survive the next revision.

For cross-page feedback, send the company domain, the chosen inputs, what you expected, and what happened.

## Review-complete signal

When the three runs are complete, say **review complete**. Codex will then:

1. reconcile your comments with Cursor's claims and the 46-item acceptance matrix;
2. run the independent Q&A and any targeted reproduction tests;
3. apply the 100-point scorecard and score caps;
4. report what Cursor did best, what missed the contract, what your review changed, and the recommended correction plan;
5. keep release work paused unless you separately authorize it.
